\abstract
{The mass--luminosity relation (MLR) of low-mass main-sequence stars links
observed luminosities to stellar masses throughout Galactic and exoplanet
astronomy, yet its metallicity dependence is calibrated almost entirely by
stellar-evolution models.  Wide binaries resolved by \gaia\ carry dynamical
information on the sum of their component masses through their projected
relative velocities, and their components share a common birth composition.}
{We aim to measure, directly from the orbital motions of \gaia\ wide binaries,
how the main-sequence MLR in the \gaia\ $G$ band depends on metallicity, and
to quantify the departures of the inferred relation from the PARSEC models.}
{We analyse 14\,876 main-sequence--main-sequence wide binaries within 1~kpc.
A modular Bayesian model first combines per-star metallicities inferred from
\gaia\ XP spectra with the PARSEC colour--magnitude relation to obtain a
posterior for the shared metallicity of each system.  A second module models
the stellar mass as a hard-monotone tensor-product B-spline surface in
absolute magnitude and metallicity, expressed relative to the PARSEC
prediction, and confronts it with the mass-scaled projected-velocity statistic
$u=\Delta v_\perp\sqrt{r_\perp}$ through a Rice-convolved likelihood with an
outlier component.  The shape of the orbital-velocity distribution is sampled
jointly with the MLR in four independent metallicity bins.}
{The inferred MLR depends significantly on metallicity.  Relative to PARSEC,
stars in the most metal-rich bin ($\mh=0.3$--$0.6$) are $8$--$14$\% more
massive at $5\le M_G\le13$, while those in the most metal-poor bin
($\mh=-1.0$--$-0.5$) are $3$--$6$\% less massive for $M_G\lesssim11$ and
$\approx20$\% less massive at $M_G=13$.  Near-solar bins agree with PARSEC to
within $\approx7$\% for $M_G\lesssim9$ but fall $10$--$18$\% below it at
$M_G=13$.  The metallicity sensitivity $\dd\ln M/\dd\mh$ rises from
$0.27\pm0.04$ at $M_G=5$ to $0.75\pm0.09$ at $M_G=13$, 1.4--1.8 times the
PARSEC value.  When the velocity-distribution shape is instead fixed to its
orbital-population prediction, the inferred masses exceed PARSEC by
$\approx30$\% at $M_G\approx8$; the absolute mass scale is therefore
controlled by the assumed velocity distribution.}
{Within the adopted family of velocity distributions, wide-binary dynamics
favour a metallicity dependence of the main-sequence MLR with the sign
predicted by stellar models, metal-rich stars being more massive at fixed
$M_G$, but with a larger amplitude than PARSEC.  Both the absolute mass scale
and the metallicity sensitivity are degenerate with the
intrinsic velocity distribution of wide binaries, whose posterior lies at the
edge of the explored range; an independent constraint on that distribution is
the main requirement for a model-independent dynamical calibration.}
